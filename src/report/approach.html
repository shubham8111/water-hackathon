Question

Hackathon Theme 2, Question 3: can we use past rainfall and reservoir storage data to forecast end-of-monsoon reservoir storage, and how early can engineers make a reasonably informed estimate? We answer it for Krishnaraja Sagar (KRS) on the Cauvery, taking 30 September as the end of the south-west monsoon.

Storage on 30 Sep = storage today + inflow still to come − releases still to come − evaporation, capped at capacity. So we built two things:

  1. A daily inflow model that learns how much inflow, and with what delay, follows rain on the watershed and releases from the two upstream dams (Harangi and Hemavathy).
  2. A direct end-of-season model that, on a given forecast date, predicts 30-Sep storage from what is known that day. It is scored by how its error shrinks as the season goes on.

Because rainfall ends in 2025, all models are trained and evaluated on 1991–2025. Reservoir data for 2026 is used only for the storage-only forecast example.

Step 1: cleaning (src/prepare.py)

  • Find the header row in each reservoir file (Harangi has 3 title rows above it). Parse dates as DD-MM-YYYY and drop duplicates.
  • Re-index to a continuous daily calendar (each file was missing one day).
  • Storage above design capacity (KRS 49.45, Hemavathy 37.10, Harangi 8.50 TMC, more than 1% over) is impossible. It is set to missing.
  • Single-day storage spikes (a jump over 3 TMC that reverses the next day) are set to missing.
  • Zero inflow during June–September is treated as a missing reading. Zero inflow outside the monsoon is kept.
  • Gaps of 3 days or less are linearly interpolated. Longer gaps stay missing.
  • A water-balance check (storage change vs inflow − outflow) flags days with residuals above 1 TMC. They are reported but not changed.
  • Rainfall: build a 0.25° box around each grid point and intersect it with the watershed. The 24 cells with any overlap are kept, each weighted by the fraction of its area inside. Basin rainfall is the area-weighted mean. Cells are also split into three equal-count distance bands (near, mid, far) by straight-line distance to the KRS dam.

Every issue and action is written to outputs/data_fixes.json and listed on the Data fixes tab.

Step 2: daily inflow model (src/inflow_model.py)

Target: log(1 + KRS inflow) on day t. Inflow is very skewed, so the model learns on a log scale. Predictions are converted back with a smearing correction (Duan) so total volume is not biased low.

Inputs (all known by the end of day t):

  • Rain in each distance band on days t to t−7.
  • Basin rain totals over the last 3, 7, 14, 30 and 60 days.
  • Soil wetness: an antecedent precipitation index (APIt = k·APIt−1 + raint, k = 0.9 and 0.97), and rain since 1 June.
  • Harangi and Hemavathy releases on days t to t−5, and their % full.
  • Day of year as sine and cosine.
  • Optional: KRS inflow on days t−1 to t−7 (the "+ past KRS inflow" variant).

Models: scikit-learn HistGradientBoostingRegressor (600 trees, learning rate 0.04, 31 leaves, at least 30 samples per leaf). Baselines: day-of-year average and "same as yesterday".

Validation: split by time, never at random. Train 1991–2014, check on 2015–2019, then refit on 1991–2019 and test once on 2020–2025. Scores: Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency (NSE), Kling–Gupta efficiency (KGE), RMSE, MAE and volume bias.

Amount and delay: a separate, readable linear model, fitted on June–October days. KRS inflow = Σk=0..10 ak·rainband(t−k) + bk·rainband(t−k)·wetness + ck·release(t−k). Coefficients are constrained to be non-negative (Ridge, positive=True). The a and b curves give cusecs per mm on dry and wet soil, and c gives the share of an upstream release that reaches KRS k days later.

What the model relies on: the test-set NSE drop when each group of inputs is shuffled (permutation importance, 3 repeats).

Step 3: 30-Sep storage forecast (src/season_forecast.py)

  • One row per (year, forecast date). Forecast dates: 1 and 15 June, July, August and September.
  • Target: storage change from the forecast date to 30 Sep, added back to today's storage and capped at capacity. Two versions: KRS alone, and all three dams combined. The combined version counts water still held upstream, so upstream releases don't need to be forecast.
  • Inputs: storage today, inflow since 1 June (each dam and combined), KRS inflow and releases over the last 7 days, KRS releases since 1 June, basin and far-band rain since 1 June, rain in the last 14 days, and May rainfall.
  • Models: Ridge (α = 5, standardised inputs) and shallow gradient boosting, each fitted separately for each forecast date. Baselines: the long-term average 30-Sep storage, and storage today plus the average change from that date.
  • Validation: leave-one-year-out over 1991–2025: each year is forecast by a model that never saw it. The 80% range comes from the 10th and 90th percentile of those held-out errors for each forecast date.

Step 4: peak analysis (src/peak_analysis.py)

  • Peak events: observed daily maxima in 2020–2025 that are above the 1991–2019 95th percentile and the largest value within ±7 days.
  • Peak-day error: each model's prediction for the peak day itself, as % of the observed peak.
  • Timing: days from the observed peak to the model's highest value within ±3 days.
  • Rise capture: on days when inflow rose by at least 5,000 cusecs, the share of that rise the model predicted on the same day.
  • High-flow detection: day-by-day hits, misses and false alarms above the 95th and 99th percentile, giving probability of detection, false-alarm ratio and critical success index (CSI).
  • Volume bias by flow band: predicted vs observed volume within bands of observed inflow.

Reproduce it

# Python 3.12; from the project root
uv venv .venv -p 3.12
uv pip install -p .venv -r requirements.txt

.venv/bin/python src/prepare.py          # clean data      -> data/processed/, outputs/data_fixes.json
.venv/bin/python src/inflow_model.py     # daily model     -> outputs/inflow_results.json
.venv/bin/python src/season_forecast.py  # 30-Sep forecast -> outputs/season_results.json
.venv/bin/python src/peak_analysis.py    # peaks           -> outputs/peak_results.json
.venv/bin/python src/export_dashboard.py # tidy tables     -> outputs/dash/*.json
.venv/bin/python src/build_report.py     # this page       -> outputs/KRS_inflow_report.html

All random seeds are fixed (random_state=0), so a rerun gives the same numbers. The report is one self-contained HTML file with d3 v7.9.0 and the data embedded. It opens offline.

Limitations and next steps

  • Releases from KRS are operator decisions (irrigation, interstate water-sharing obligations). Rainfall cannot predict them. The forecast assumes they follow historical patterns.
  • Upstream canal diversions (Hemavathy and Harangi canals) are not in the data, so the combined 3-dam view is not a closed water balance.
  • Forecasts made before August carry little skill because most of the season's rain has not fallen. Next step: run each forecast with every past year's remaining-season rainfall through the daily model, giving a range of outcomes. Or use IMD extended-range forecasts.
  • Distance bands are a proxy for travel time. A DEM-based flow-length or sub-catchment delineation (SRTM 30 m) would be more physical.
  • No evaporation input. MODIS/PML evapotranspiration (listed in the hackathon data sources) could be added.
